FOUNDATION

Extended Electrodynamics & SLW

Classical Electrodynamics (CED) and QED gauge away the longitudinal (irrotational) components of the vector potential A, electric field E, and current density J. Experiments (Aharonov-Bohm, Maxwell-Lodge effect, Varma et al., living cell ion currents, lightning) demonstrate these components exist and are physically measurable.

Extended Electrodynamics (EED) restores completeness by retaining:

  • The scalar field C = ∇·A + (1/c²) ∂Φ/∂t (non-zero, dynamical)
  • Longitudinal electric field E_L and gradient-driven current J_L = ∇κ
  • Scalar-Longitudinal Waves (SLW) that propagate with B ≈ 0

Because B=0, there are no eddy currents → SLW is unconstrained by the classical skin effect and can propagate through thousands of skin depths of solid copper (preliminary experimental confirmation in Hively & Loebl 2019).

SLW Transmitter (Monopole + Skirt Balun)
A linear monopole driven at frequency f creates an oscillating charge distribution. The coaxial skirt balun (λ/4 length) cancels return current on the outer conductor by 180° phase shift, forcing a pure gradient-driven current J_L = ∇κ on the radiating element. This launches the SLW (isotropic, ~1/r² power density).

Bifilar Coil Detector (~palm size)
Flat bifilar spiral (alternating conductors, ~10 cm diameter, wire-thin thickness). Adjacent turns carry current in opposite directions → net B ≈ 0 (magnetic cancellation). The coil is oriented face-on to the incoming SLW so the longitudinal E_L component drives a measurable gradient current between the two conductors. Output is taken differentially or single-ended into 50 Ω.

How the Bifilar Coil Produces Measurable Output Current

  1. The incident SLW carries a longitudinal electric field E_L (radial for spherical wave, approx. uniform across the small detector at long range) and associated scalar field C.
  2. The palm-sized bifilar coil (diameter ≈ 10 cm, flat spiral) is oriented with its plane perpendicular to the propagation direction (maximum projected area ≈ 78.5 cm² facing the transmitter).
  3. Because adjacent turns are wound in opposite sense and conductively coupled, any magnetic field component is cancelled to first order (B ≈ 0). There is therefore no classical skin-effect dissipation.
  4. The longitudinal E_L drives charges along the wire length in a gradient fashion. The differential voltage/current between the two interleaved conductors is extracted as the output signal.
  5. This output is modeled here as delivering power P_load = S(r) × A_eff × η into a 50 Ω load. The resulting rms current I_rms = √(P_load / 50) is what would be presented to the front-end of a modern EW receiver.

Note: Actual conversion efficiency depends on exact winding geometry, wire conductivity, matching network, and frequency. The 50% figure used is conservative for educational illustration and consistent with the order-of-magnitude laboratory results shown in the Hively patent figures.
